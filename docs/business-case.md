# Gapwise: business case

Founder: Dara Whitfield. Written 2026-11-02. Where I do not know a number, it says ASK.

## B1. The problem

Independent dental practices lose money every week to last-minute cancellations. The front-desk person phones the waiting list one patient at a time, gets voicemail, and the chair stays empty. I did this job for six years at one practice and lost about five slots a week. That is one practice, so it is my experience, not yet a fact about all practices.

How they cope today: a sticky note or a spreadsheet nobody updates, and phone calls. Value of one filled slot to a practice: ASK (a cleaning is about $110, a crown about $400). Share of cancelled slots that can be refilled at all: ASK.

## B2. What I sell

Gapwise. The practice sends us the day's cancellation. We text the waiting patients straight away and the first to say yes gets the slot. The practice does nothing else. In the first months I run the texting by hand; nothing is built.

What we promise: we try, and we show every result. We do not promise a filled chair.

## B3. Who buys, how many, and the target

The office manager or owner-dentist at an independent practice with 1 to 4 dentists, starting in the Portland area. We reach them by cold email and LinkedIn.

- How many such practices there are: ASK (I counted a state directory but have not cleaned it).
- Target: 12 paying practices by 2027-05-31, which is $1,788 a month. That is about 2 new practices a month from December 2026 to May 2027.
- Whether texting waiting patients needs written consent in every state: ASK (needs a lawyer or a practice that knows).

## B4. Price and what is left

- Price: $149 a month per practice, month to month, no contract, no setup fee. A practice can stop whenever it wants, so keeping practices (churn) is a real unknown.
- Cost to deliver: $22 per practice per month ($14 texts, $5 card fees, $3 support tools). My own time is about 1.5 hours per practice per month and is not charged.
- Money left per practice after delivery: $127 (85% of the price).

## B5. Costs and winning a customer

- Fixed costs: $240 a month (texting base $49, website and tools $99, booking $22, insurance and accounts $70). Two practices cover them.
- Cost to win one paying practice: ASK (my guess is $300, which is about $60 per booked call and about 5 calls per paying practice).
- At 12 practices: $1,788 in, $264 delivery, $240 fixed, $1,284 left a month.

## B6. My time and what I need to earn

- 20 hours a week on Gapwise, about 87 a month. The other 20 hours a week are my contract bookkeeping.
- Living costs I need the business to cover: ASK. Until I answer, my hours are not turned into cash anywhere in the model.

## B7. Cash

- Cash I can put in: $9,000. No outside money.

## B8. Kill criterion and what I am least sure of

If fewer than 4 practices are paying $149 a month by 2027-02-28, I stop spending cash and go back to full-time bookkeeping.

What I am least sure of: that a practice can hand over its cancellations without custom work (assumption 3, which blocks the others), and that patients will answer fast enough to fill a chair (assumption 5).

## Hypothesis register

Nine things that must be true. Evidence is what I have today; most is thin.

| # | What must be true | Type | What I have today |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Office managers at independent dental practices lose at least 3 appointment slots a week to last-minute cancellations. | Desirability | My six years at one practice (about 5 lost slots a week). One practice only. |
| 2 | Independent dental practices will pay $149 a month to have cancelled slots filled for them. | Viability | None. Nobody has been asked to pay yet. |
| 3 | A practice can give us its daily cancellation list (by file or by copy) without any custom software work. This blocks the others. | Feasibility | My old practice software had an export; practices use many programs and none has been checked. |
| 4 | Waiting patients accept offer texts: no more than 3 in 100 opt out. | Desirability | Patients at my old practice agreed to reminder texts. Nobody has been asked about offer texts. |
| 5 | Waiting patients take a day-of offer fast enough that at least 1 in 3 cancelled slots is filled within 2 hours. | Desirability | None. My guess from the waiting list at my old practice. |
| 6 | We can reach office managers by cold email and LinkedIn for $60 or less per booked call. | Viability | Two replies to a LinkedIn post. Nothing measured. |
| 7 | We can set up a new practice in under 2 hours. | Feasibility | I did the setup once at my old practice in about 3 hours. |
| 8 | At least 8 in 10 practices that start are still paying in month 3. | Viability | None. Needs three months of paying practices. |
| 9 | Front-desk staff see the service as relief, not a threat to their jobs. (From my mission and values.) | Desirability | Three former colleagues said they would like it. Friendly, not tested. |

## What happens next

Round 1 (2026-11-09 to 2026-12-07) runs two experiments: will office managers pay (assumptions 2 and 6), and can a slot be filled by hand-run texting in a pilot with three practices (assumptions 3, 4 and 5).
