# Dara Whitfield: Ikigai

Written 2026-11-02 for Gapwise. Plain words, my own story. Where I do not know, it says ASK.

## What I love

- Making a hard morning at the front desk easier for the person living it.
- Small, independent practices where people know each other's names and the office manager also orders the gloves.
- Fixing a messy daily job with a simple routine, and then watching it hold up.
- Working 20 focused hours a week on something I can see helping, next to my 20 hours of steady contract bookkeeping.

## What I am good at

- Six years running a dental front desk: the schedule, the waiting list, the phone tag, the sticky note.
- Talking to office managers as one of them, so they tell me the truth about a bad week.
- Numbers and accounts from my bookkeeping work: I keep the costs honest ($22 to deliver, $240 fixed a month).
- Doing a job by hand, one patient at a time, before anything is built.

## What the world needs

- Practices that lose about 5 slots a week to cancellations (from my own practice; one practice only, so it still needs checking).
- Front-desk people who can finish on time instead of calling voicemail all morning.
- Independent practices that stay independent. How many there are around Portland: ASK.
- Texts that respect patients: only people who agreed (consent rules in every state: ASK).

## What I can be paid for

- A practice pays $149 a month, month to month, to have cancelled slots filled for it. Nobody has paid yet; this is the biggest open question.
- Delivery costs $22 a practice, so $127 is left from each; two practices cover the $240 fixed costs.
- What I need to earn from this: ASK (living costs). The $9,000 I can put in is a test budget, not a wage.

## The four overlaps

- **Passion** (love and good at): helping front-desk teams with a job I know in my hands.
- **Mission** (love and the world needs): keeping small practices' chairs full and their staff on time.
- **Vocation** (good at and paid for): a front-desk specialist who can run the text-and-fill routine, and bookkeeping to keep the numbers straight.
- **Profession** (the world needs and paid for): filling empty chairs is worth real money to a practice (a cleaning about $110, a crown about $400).

Where all four meet: a small, plain-priced service for independent practices that fills cancelled slots by text.

## Direction

I should build Gapwise for independent dental practices with 1 to 4 dentists in Portland, not for chains. It uses what I learned in six years at a front desk, serves people like the office managers I used to work beside, and has to earn its keep at $149 a month per practice. I will test it with the time I have: 20 hours a week, $9,000, and a stop line of fewer than 4 paying practices by 2027-02-28, after which I go back to full-time bookkeeping. This supports the mission (full chairs, finished on time, independent practices) and the business case (12 paying practices by 2027-05-31). What I still have to learn: whether practices will pay, whether they can hand over their cancellations, and whether patients answer fast enough.
