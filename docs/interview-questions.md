# Gapwise: interview questions

Draft for Dara Whitfield, built from the Waya interview-questions template and the Gapwise business case. Status: draft, not yet approved by the founder.

## Rules

1. Past behaviour only. Ask what happened ("the last time"), never what they would do.
2. No pitch. Do not describe Gapwise until the problem and today's workaround are on the table. Say what you are doing in one sentence and stop.
3. One question at a time. Ask it, then stay quiet.
4. Compliments are not evidence. Time, data or money are.
5. Pass line first, written before the first call.

## Who and which assumptions

- Who this is for: the office manager or owner-dentist at an independent dental practice with 1 to 4 dentists, Portland area.
- Assumptions these calls inform: 1 (office managers lose at least 3 slots a week to last-minute cancellations) and 3 (a practice can give us its daily cancellation list without custom work). The consent question also feeds assumption 4 (no more than 3 in 100 patients opt out).
- Pass line: at least 4 of 6 people describe losing slots without me raising it, and at least 3 of 6 say how they could hand over a list (file, copy or email). A person who has paid or spent time on the problem in the last 3 months counts double.

## The eight phases, in this order

**1. Set-up (2 minutes)**
- "I am not selling anything. May I record so I can listen properly?"
- "I am trying to understand how a dental practice handles last-minute cancellations. There are no wrong answers."

**2. Context**
- "Tell me about your role and your week."
- "Who else is involved when a patient cancels: the front desk, the hygienist, the dentist?"
- "How many chairs and dentists do you have?"

**3. Open pain**
- "What is the hardest part of keeping the schedule full?"
- "What gets in the way most often?"

**4. Last time it happened**
- "Tell me about the last time a patient cancelled on the same day. Walk me through it."
- "What happened next? Who did you call? Who answered?"
- "How many cancellations did you have last week? How many of those were same-day?"

**5. Current solution**
- "How do you fill a cancelled slot now? Where is the waiting list kept?"
- "What have you tried? What did you stop using, and why?"

**6. Cost and urgency**
- "What did the last empty slot cost the practice, roughly?"
- "What happens if cancellations are not handled better this quarter?"
- "Have you spent money or time trying to fix this?"

**7. Buying process**
- "What did you last buy for the front desk or the schedule? Who approved it?"
- "What can you approve alone, and where does the owner-dentist step in?"
- "How do you decide on a monthly bill like that?"
- For the office manager: "Who signs off on anything with a bill?"

**8. Close**
- "Could I come back when there is something to try?"
- "Could you show me an example from your own work, for instance last week's cancellations?"
- "Who else should I talk to?"

## Practice details to find out (ask in phases 4 to 7, past tense)

- How the practice software shows cancellations: a file, a screen, or an email. "Last time you needed a list of the day's changes, how did you get it?"
- Whether patients signed a form for texts, and what the form covers. "What do patients sign about reminders? When was it last updated?" Only ask what the form says. Do not give legal advice.

## Ask for something

Ask for the strongest next step that is fair, and log the one they actually gave: another call, an introduction, an example of their cancellation list, a signed letter of intent, a pre-order or a first month paid.

## Solution interview for the pilot (after the problem is on the table)

Only after phases 1 to 7. Use it with people who have agreed to the pilot or are about to. One idea at a time.

1. "Here is what I would do: you send me the day's cancellations, I text the waiting patients, the first to say yes gets the slot. Is anything in that different from how you pictured it?" (one sentence, then stop)
2. "Looking at last week's cancellations, which ones could have been handled that way, and which could not?"
3. "Walk me through how you would send me the list tomorrow morning. Where would you click, and how long would it take?"
4. "Which patients on your waiting list agreed to texts? What did they agree to?"
5. "Who at your practice needs to say yes before we start? What did they ask for the last time you proposed something like this?"
6. "The price is $149 a month, month to month. When you last started a monthly service, what made you decide?"
7. "Is there anything that would make you stop texting patients through this?"

Never promise a filled chair. Say: "We try, and we show every result."

## Debrief template (within 24 hours of each call)

- Date, guest, practice, source (cold email, LinkedIn).
- Did the call work? (good audio, enough time) Yes / No. A failed call does not count as a conversation about the product.
- Slots lost last week, and how many same-day: ____
- What they do today to fill slots: ____
- Value of a slot in their words: ____
- How they could hand over the list (file, copy, email, cannot): ____
- Texting consent: what patients signed, who checks: ____
- Who signs off on a bill: ____
- Strongest next step they gave (another call, introduction, example, payment): ____
- Their words I want to keep (one or two lines, exact): ____
- Did it match the pass line? Which assumption does it support or weaken? ____
